\subsection*{Ethics statement}
This work studies efficient multi-turn LLM serving and does not involve human subjects, user studies, or the collection of new personal data. All experiments use publicly available dialogue and reasoning benchmarks under their respective licenses (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_dataset}), and we release no new dataset. SOMA is intended to reduce the cost and latency of serving LLMs, which can broaden access to interactive LLM systems and reduce their energy footprint. As with any method that substitutes a smaller surrogate for a larger model, there is a risk of quality degradation when a conversation drifts away from the adapted local region, which is most consequential in high-stakes domains such as medical, legal, or financial assistance. SOMA mitigates this risk through its semantic acceptance gate and drift-aware rollback, but real deployments should retain monitoring, conservative switching thresholds, and human oversight where errors may cause harm. Because SOMA adapts to dialogue history, deployments should also follow data-minimization and retention practices to protect user privacy. Broader impacts and limitations are discussed further in Appendices~\ref{sec:broader_impact} and~\ref{sec:limitations}.

For the theoretical results in Section~\ref{sec:theory}, all assumptions are stated explicitly and complete proofs are provided in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_proof}, with the supporting mathematical background in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_math}.
