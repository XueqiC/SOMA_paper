Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes SOMA in four steps. (1) The original model \(F\) serves the first \(W\) turns and thereby establishes the dialogue state. \rev{(2) Adversarial soft-prompt mining measures, for each warm-start turn, how much of the agreement between the surrogate \(G\) and \(F\) survives a perturbation designed to remove it (Sections~\ref{sec:init}--\ref{sec:mining}). (3) A lightweight LoRA adapter distills \(F\)'s responses, weighted by this score,} and (4) a semantic gate hands the session to \(G\), with rollback to \(F\) when the dialogue leaves the learned region (Section~\ref{sec:ft-switch}).

\subsection{Initialization}\label{sec:init}

We initialize a soft prompt $\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_p\times d}$ on the surrogate $G$ by sampling each row i.i.d.\ from $\boldsymbol{p}_{\ell}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},\sigma^2\mathbf{I}_d)$, where $\sigma>0$ sets the initialization scale, and let $\mathbf{E}=[\boldsymbol e_v]_{v\in\mathcal V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times |\mathcal V|}$ be the surrogate embedding matrix\rev{, which also defines the semantic neighborhoods used below}.

\rev{With $\mathcal D_{t-1}$ the text history before turn $t$ and $q_t$ the user query, the mining target is the response that $F$ already produced while serving the warm-start turn, and the surrogate is probed with the soft prompt prepended at its embedding layer:}
\[
a_t^{F}=F\!\left(\mathcal D_{t-1}\oplus q_t\right),
\qquad
 a_t^{G}=G\!\left(\mathbf P\oplus_{\mathrm{emb}}
\mathbf E(\mathrm{tok}_G(\mathcal D_{t-1}\oplus q_t))\right).
\]
Here $\mathrm{tok}_G(\cdot)$ is the tokenizer of $G$, $\mathbf E(\cdot)$ maps tokens to embeddings, and $\oplus_{\mathrm{emb}}$ concatenates along the sequence. \rev{Both models see the same dialogue state, and only the surrogate is perturbed: $F$ is called once per warm-start turn, never with the prompt, its responses stay fixed during optimization, and gradients flow only through $\mathbf P$ on $G$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_rationale}).}

\subsection{\rev{Adversarial Soft-Prompt Mining}}\label{sec:mining}

We learn $\mathbf P$ by minimizing a differentiable objective that pushes $G$ away from $F$ on the local dialogue context. The objective has three parts: a token-level semantic divergence loss, an expectation-weighted distribution-level term, and an anti-degeneration regularizer.

\textbf{Semantic divergence loss.}
An unlikelihood-style loss~\citep{welleck2019neural} discourages $G$ from assigning high probability to the tokens produced by $F$. An exact-token penalty alone is too narrow, since $G$ could avoid the teacher token yet still favor close paraphrases, so we penalize a semantic neighborhood in the surrogate embedding space.

\begin{definition}[Semantic Neighborhood]
For token $u\in\mathcal V$, let $\mathcal N_k(u)$ be the set of the $k$ tokens in $\mathcal V\setminus\{u\}$ whose embeddings have the largest cosine similarity to $\boldsymbol e_u$.
\end{definition}

At turn $t$ the original model produces $a_t^F$, which we tokenize with the surrogate tokenizer, $\boldsymbol y_t^F=\mathrm{tok}_G(a_t^F)=(y_{t,1}^F,\ldots,y_{t,T_F(t)}^F)$, so that teacher output and surrogate distribution share one vocabulary. Each teacher token spreads temperature-weighted mass over itself and its $k$ nearest neighbors in the surrogate embedding space:
\[
s_{\tau}(v\mid y_{t,i}^F)=
\frac{\exp(\cos(\boldsymbol e_v,\boldsymbol e_{y_{t,i}^F})/\tau)}
{\sum_{u\in\{y_{t,i}^F\}\cup\mathcal N_k(y_{t,i}^F)}
\exp(\cos(\boldsymbol e_u,\boldsymbol e_{y_{t,i}^F})/\tau)},
\qquad \tau>0 .
\]
Let $S_t=\mathcal D_{t-1}\oplus q_t$. With causal masking, a single forward pass of $G$ on the prefix $S_t$ and the teacher answer prefix gives the next-token distributions
$\{\Pi_{t,i}(\mathbf P)\}_{i=1}^{T_F(t)}$, where
$\Pi_{t,i}(\mathbf P)=\pi_G(\cdot\mid S_t;\mathbf P, a_{t,<i}^F)$.
The semantic divergence loss is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sem-with-aF}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{sem}}(\mathbf P;\mathcal D_{t-1},q_t)=
\frac{1}{T_F(t)}\sum_{i=1}^{T_F(t)}
\sum_{v\in\{y_{t,i}^F\}\cup\mathcal N_k(y_{t,i}^F)}
s_{\tau}(v\mid y_{t,i}^F)
\Big[-\log\big(1-\Pi_{t,i}(\mathbf P)[v]\big)\Big].
\end{equation}
\rev{Minimizing Eq.~\ref{eq:sem-with-aF} removes the agreement of $G$ with $F$ on exact tokens and on nearby semantic alternatives, so agreement expressed through a paraphrase cannot survive the probe.}

\textbf{Expectation-weighted semantic divergence.}
The neighborhood loss can still miss distribution-level alignment: $G$ may lower the probability of a teacher token and its neighbors while spreading mass over many other tokens with similar meaning. We therefore compare the teacher token's embedding with the expected embedding of the surrogate's next-token distribution, $\bar{\boldsymbol e}_{t,i}=\mathbf E\Pi_{t,i}(\mathbf P)
=\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}\Pi_{t,i}(\mathbf P)[v] \boldsymbol e_v $. We therefore upweight the neighborhood penalty whenever the expected embedding still points toward the teacher token, using the weight $w_{t,i}(\mathbf P)=1+\lambda_{\mathrm{exp}}\,
\mathrm{clip}\!\left(
\cos(\bar{\boldsymbol e}_{t,i},\boldsymbol e_{y_{t,i}^{F}}),0,1
\right)$ with $\lambda_{\mathrm{exp}}\ge 0$. The final semantic mining loss is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:semexp-with-aF}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{sem\_exp}}(\mathbf P;\mathcal D_{t-1},q_t)=
\frac{1}{T_F(t)}\sum_{i=1}^{T_F(t)}
w_{t,i}(\mathbf P)
\sum_{v\in\{y_{t,i}^F\}\cup\mathcal N_k(y_{t,i}^F)}
s_{\tau}(v\mid y_{t,i}^{F})
\Big[-\log\big(1-\Pi_{t,i}(\mathbf P)[v]\big)\Big].
\end{equation}
Thus the loss blocks local copying through the neighborhood term and detects broader semantic shadowing through the expectation weight.

\textbf{Anti-degeneration regularizer.}
Mining can collapse if $G$ piles probability onto a few high-frequency tokens, yielding bland continuations that \rev{suppress $F$'s tokens trivially and make the per-turn scores uninformative}~\citep{li2023repetition,holtzman2019curious,meister2023locally}. We therefore preserve \rev{the entropy of $G$'s predictions over the last $K$ positions of the teacher response}; reusing the logits of the same forward pass, define
\[
H_{\mathrm{tail}}(\mathbf P;t)=
\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i\in\mathrm{tail}(t,K)}
\left[-\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}\Pi_{t,i}(\mathbf P)[v]
\log \Pi_{t,i}(\mathbf P)[v]\right].
\]
The complete prompt-mining objective for a minibatch of warm-start turns $\mathcal B$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prompt-final}
\mathcal J(\mathbf P)=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B}
\left[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{sem\_exp}}(\mathbf P;\mathcal D_{t-1},q_t)
-\beta H_{\mathrm{tail}}(\mathbf P;t)
\right]
+\lambda_P\|\mathbf P\|_F^2 .
\end{equation}
We minimize Eq.~\ref{eq:prompt-final} with AdamW and gradient clipping while all weights of $G$ stay frozen. \rev{Neighbors are precomputed once by nearest-neighbor search over normalized token embeddings}, and $\bar{\boldsymbol e}_{t,i}$ uses a top-$m$ truncation of $\Pi_{t,i}(\mathbf P)$, so the per-step overhead is linear in the number of teacher-prefix positions and scales with $k+m$ rather than with the vocabulary size $|\mathcal V|$.

\subsection{Localized Fine-Tuning, Switching, and Rollback}\label{sec:ft-switch}

\rev{Each warm-start turn receives an anchoring score, its largest residual mining loss over the candidate prompts, $r_t=\max_{\mathbf P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{cand}}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{sem\_exp}}(\mathbf P;\mathcal D_{t-1},q_t)$. Since $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{sem\_exp}}$ grows with the probability that $G$ assigns to $F$'s tokens and their neighbors, a large $r_t$ means that $G$ still predicts $F$'s response after a probe has pushed it away, so the response is anchored in the dialogue state rather than in turn-specific details. Unlike raw agreement, $r_t$ counts only the agreement that the probe cannot remove, which separates robust matches from incidental ones. The training set $\mathcal H$ holds the warm-start turns with $F$'s responses, weighted by $r_t$: anchored responses carry the topic, constraints, and style that later turns share, whereas fragile ones carry turn-specific content that can dominate a fit on $W$ examples, in the spirit of small-loss selection~\citep{han2018coteaching} and self-paced learning~\citep{kumar2010selfpaced}.}

We freeze the base weights of $G$, attach LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} to attention and MLP projections, and optimize \rev{the weighted negative log-likelihood of $F$'s responses}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lora-ft}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FT}}(\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm{LoRA}})=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal H|}\sum_{(S_t,a_t^F)\in\mathcal H}
\omega_t\,\mathrm{NLL}\big(a_t^F\mid G(S_t;\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm{LoRA}})\big),
\qquad
\omega_t\propto r_t .
\end{equation}
The soft prompts are then discarded, and serving uses only the LoRA-adapted surrogate.

Before switching, SOMA verifies two conditions: the adapted surrogate must reproduce the original model's responses on a small acceptance batch, and the current query must remain close to the warm-start dialogue centroid. Once accepted, \(G\) serves later turns from a compressed input \(\widetilde S_t\) that keeps a fixed-budget summary of earlier content together with the most recent \(K\) turns, so the full history is never re-processed. During surrogate serving, SOMA keeps monitoring semantic drift. If recent queries move away from the learned local state, it rolls back to \(F\), refreshes the summary and the centroid, and returns to \(G\) only after the acceptance gate passes again. \rev{Because the drift statistic is a continuous distance to the warm-start centroid, gradual drift raises it progressively and triggers rollback once the margin is exhausted, rather than only on abrupt shifts.}
